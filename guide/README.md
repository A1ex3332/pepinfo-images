# The Pepinfo research guide (free, sourced)

Version 1, 25 Nov 2026. By @pepinfo07 on Instagram.

**Read this first.** This is education about published research, not medical advice. Every compound below
is sold as a *research chemical*: research use only, not for human consumption, 21+. Nothing here tells you
to use, dose or take anything, and no study below proves a peptide treats, cures or prevents anything in you.
Each finding says what it was tested in: **cells**, **mice**, **rats** or **people**. Results in cells and
animals often fail in humans.

## How to read this guide

- **Model first.** "In mice" means mice. A cell result is the weakest kind of evidence; a randomised human
  trial is the strongest.
- **One study is one study.** Many of these findings come from one lab and have not been repeated.
- **Every line links to its source** on PubMed (PMID) or ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT). Click it and read the abstract.

## The compounds

### GHK-Cu (a copper peptide your blood already carries)
- First found in **human blood plasma** in 1973 (PMID [4349963](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/4349963/)).
- In **cell culture**, GHK-Cu raised collagen production by skin fibroblasts (1988, PMID [3169264](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3169264/)).
- Review of the research so far, mostly cells and animals (2015, PMID [26236730](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26236730/)).
- *Still unknown:* whether lab results carry over to people outside cosmetic skin studies.

### LL-37 (a peptide in your sweat)
- Found in **human sweat**, made by sweat glands as part of the skin's defences (2002, PMID [12445197](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12445197/)).
- Also made by the **eye's surface cells**, where it killed microbes in **lab tests** (2005, PMID [16020269](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16020269/)).
- *Still unknown:* what lab-made LL-37 does outside the lab.

### Kisspeptin (the puberty signal)
- People born with a broken kisspeptin receptor (GPR54) **did not go through puberty normally** (human genetics, 2003, PMID [12944565](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12944565/) and [14573733](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14573733/)).
- Blood levels rise sharply in **human pregnancy** (2003, PMID [12574233](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12574233/)).
- *Still unknown:* long-term effects of giving it from outside; trials are small.

### MOTS-c (a peptide coded in your mitochondria)
- Exercise raised MOTS-c levels in **people**, and in **old mice** it improved physical performance (2021, PMID [33473109](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33473109/)).
- Under stress it moved into the cell nucleus and switched genes on, in **cells** (2018, PMID [29983246](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29983246/)).
- *Still unknown:* no published human trial of lab-made MOTS-c.

### SS-31 / elamipretide (a mitochondria-targeted peptide)
- Improved muscle energy and performance in **aged mice** (2013, PMID [23692570](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23692570/)).
- Tested in **people with Barth syndrome**, a rare mitochondrial disease, in an open-label extension (2024, PMID [38602181](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38602181/)).
- *Still unknown:* any effect in healthy people.

### Epitalon (a 4-amino-acid pineal peptide)
- Raised telomerase activity in **human cells in a dish** (2003, PMID [12937682](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12937682/)).
- Changed ageing markers in **female mice** (2003, PMID [14501183](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14501183/)).
- *Still unknown:* nearly all work comes from one research group; independent human trials are missing.

### Thymosin Alpha-1 (from your thymus)
- Your thymus shrinks with age in **humans** (2000 review, PMID [11339360](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11339360/)).
- Blood levels measured in large groups of **healthy adults** (1992, PMID [1566283](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1566283/)).
- *Still unknown:* what lab-sold versions do; they are not the licensed drug.

### TB-500 (based on thymosin beta-4)
- Thymosin beta-4 sped up wound closure in **rats** (1999, PMID [10469335](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10469335/)).
- Supported heart-cell survival in **mice** (2004, PMID [15565145](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15565145/)).
- *Still unknown:* TB-500 is a fragment, and most studies used the full protein.

### KPV (the tail of a skin hormone)
- Reduced gut inflammation in **mice** (2008, PMID [18061177](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18061177/) and [18092346](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18092346/)).
- *Still unknown:* no human trials.

### Selank (based on tuftsin, a peptide from your antibodies)
- Tuftsin was found in 1970 as a natural peptide that helps immune cells eat microbes (PMID [4097539](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/4097539/)).
- Changed GABA-related gene activity in **rat brain** (2016, PMID [26924987](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26924987/)).
- *Still unknown:* human evidence is mostly small Russian studies.

### Semax (a piece of a stress hormone)
- Built from a fragment of ACTH; raised BDNF levels in **rat hippocampus** (2006, PMID [16996037](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16996037/)).
- Studied in **stroke patients** in Russia (2018, PMID [29798983](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29798983/)).
- *Still unknown:* no large Western randomised trials.

### ARA-290 / cibinetide (a piece of the blood hormone EPO)
- In a small **human trial** of people with sarcoidosis nerve-fibre loss, it improved symptoms and corneal nerve fibre density versus placebo (2013, PMID [24136731](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24136731/)).
- A follow-up **human trial** found more corneal nerve fibres (2017, PMID [28475703](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28475703/)). Phase 2 trial record: [NCT02039687](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT02039687).
- *Still unknown:* a later phase 2 eye trial was terminated ([NCT06626971](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06626971)).

### VIP (vasoactive intestinal peptide, from your gut and brain)
- **Mice** born without VIP had disrupted daily body-clock rhythms (2014, PMID [25252712](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25252712/)).
- In a large **human trial** in hospital COVID patients, IV VIP did **not** improve recovery versus placebo (2023, PMID [37348524](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37348524/)).
- *Still unknown:* most positive results are animal data.

### DSIP (delta sleep-inducing peptide)
- Found in 1977; decades later scientists still call it "a still unresolved riddle" (2006 review, PMID [16539679](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16539679/)).

## How to read a COA (lab report) in 60 seconds

1. **Lab name.** Is it a real third-party lab (for example Janoshik, Freedom Diagnostics), not the seller?
2. **Batch or lot number.** It should match the number on the vial label.
3. **Date.** Recent, ideally within the last 12 months.
4. **Purity by HPLC** and **identity by mass spec (MS)**. Purity alone doesn't prove it is the right compound.
5. **Verify it.** Some labs give a key you can check on the lab's own site.

## Red flags in any seller
- No COA, or one COA reused for every batch.
- Claims that a product treats a disease, or dosing instructions on the product page.
- "Human grade" or "for personal use" wording on a research chemical.
- Prices far below every other shop.
