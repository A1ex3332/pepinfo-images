The COA checklist

8 things to check on any research peptide certificate of analysis before you trust it.

  1. A named, independent labThe report names the testing lab, with an address or website, and it isn't the seller testing itself.
  2. Batch number matches the vialThe lot or batch number on the COA is the same one printed on your vial's label.
  3. A recent test dateThe report is dated, and the date fits that batch. A years-old report for a new batch tells you nothing.
  4. Identity test (mass spec)Mass spectrometry shows a measured mass that matches the peptide's known molecular weight.
  5. Purity by HPLC, with the chartA purity percentage plus the HPLC chromatogram it came from. One tall main peak is what you're looking for.
  6. Amount per vialSome reports also measure how many mg are actually in the vial, not just how pure it is.
  7. A way to verify itMany labs let you check a report on their own site with a code or QR. Use it.
  8. One report per batchEach batch has its own COA. The same PDF reused for every batch is not a test of yours.

Red flags

  • No lab name, or a lab you can't find online
  • Blurry, cropped or edited-looking numbers
  • Purity of exactly 100.00%
  • No date, no batch number, or no chromatogram
  • The seller won't show the COA until you've paid
